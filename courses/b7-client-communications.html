Part 1 · covers the routine texts

The three routine texts 6 min live

Every engagement runs on the same three small texts, each with a fixed shape and a fixed rhythm. Learn the shapes once and AI becomes genuinely useful; skip them and AI produces fluent, shapeless mail.

LiveThe shapes, and where AI actually helps4 min▶

The weekly status note is four lines: done, next, risks, asks. Not a narrative, not a mood - a gauge the client can read in thirty seconds. The steering pre-read is the b5 pyramid in miniature: the answer in the first sentence, support beneath, the ask explicit - sent early enough that the meeting argues with the recommendation instead of hearing it for the first time. A pre-read that buries the ask wastes the committee. The meeting follow-up lands within 24 hours and contains exactly three things: decisions made, owners named, dates attached. It is the version of the meeting everyone works from afterward.

AI's role differs per text, and the division is the same one this whole course teaches:

TextAI drafts instantlyOnly you can supply
Weekly status notethe four-line structure from your bullet notesthe risks you are actually worried about, and the asks
Steering pre-readthe pyramid-in-miniature from your findingsthe answer you will sign, and the political sequencing
Meeting follow-updecisions/owners/dates from your meeting notesthe accuracy of every owner, and the name at the bottom

Notice the right column never changes character: judgment, politics, accountability. Feed AI your actual bullets and it produces a strong skeleton in seconds. Ask it to invent the content and it produces confident filler - which a client can smell by week 2.

Self-studyWhy the rhythm matters as much as the text2 min▶

A status note that arrives every Monday at the same hour builds a kind of trust no single brilliant email can: the client stops wondering what is happening, because the gauge always reads. Miss two Mondays and every silence becomes a signal. The pre-read's clock matters for the opposite reason - sent two days ahead, it converts meeting time from information transfer to decision making; sent the night before, it converts the committee into an audience. And the follow-up's 24-hour window exists because memory of a meeting decays fastest in the first day - the first written version becomes the truth. Put the three clocks in your calendar as recurring blocks; the texts then write themselves on schedule.

Part 2 · covers voice

Voice is a client-anchor problem 8 min live

The b5 measures apply to email too. A status note with no client specifics reads like a template because it is one. Voice is not a mystery: it is your firm's register plus the client's vocabulary - and both are things AI does not have unless you feed them in.

LiveThe default voice, and how to replace it5 min▶

You know the AI default email voice on sight: "I hope this email finds you well... I wanted to circle back and touch base regarding our previous discussion..." It is recognizable slop - and the recipient's own AI probably wrote half the mail in their inbox in the same register, which is precisely why it signals nothing. The b5 scorer would price it instantly: no answer in line one, zero numbers, weasels everywhere, and not a single client anchor.

Voice, defined operationally: your firm's register (how direct, how formal, how warm Northbridge sounds) plus the client's vocabulary. Cadence's team says "seats" and "pilots" - so you write in their nouns, not in "user licenses" and "trial deployments". Writing in the client's own vocabulary is the email version of the scorer's client-anchor measure: it proves a specific human wrote this for a specific reader.

The fix is the same division as b6: feed your actual bullets in, let AI draft the structure, edit the draft into your register, and never send unread. The edit pass is not polish - it is where the voice enters. A sent email is a signed deliverable with your name at the bottom.

Real worldThe jagged-frontier study found AI-assisted output homogenized - consultants' answers converged on similar ideas. Email is where you experience that finding daily: every AI-drafted note sounds like every other AI-drafted note. In a world where baseline production is table stakes, a status note in a recognizable human voice with the client's own numbers in it is now a differentiator - which is a strange sentence, but here we are.
Self-studyThe read-aloud test2 min▶

The cheapest voice check ever devised: read the draft aloud, and if you would not say the sentence to the client's face, delete it. "I hope this email finds you well" fails instantly - nobody has ever said that to a face. "Per my previous correspondence" fails. "The $30 tier is the answer, and here are the three numbers" passes, because you would say exactly that across a table. The test works because your speaking voice is the register the client actually knows you by; email that matches it reads as you. Run it on every AI draft before the send button - it takes thirty seconds and catches what no grammar check can.

Part 3 · covers difficult news

Difficult news, drafted with care 6 min live

The week-5 scenario: Cadence's CEO has been championing a cheaper $15 tier all engagement, and your analysis says it is wrong - churn concentrates exactly where a cheap tier would sell. Individual plans retain at 71% against 94% for enterprise-style seats; a $15 tier acquires more of the customers Cadence already loses, while competitors price at $38-45 and the $30 recommendation lifts ARPU 2.1x. Somebody has to say so, in writing, this week.

LiveThe three companions, and AI as rehearsal partner4 min▶

The discipline: bad news never travels alone. It travels with (1) the evidence - the retention math laid out so the reader can check it, not a verdict to take on faith; (2) the alternative - the $30 tier, so the email closes a door and opens one in the same breath; and (3) the respect of directness - weasel cushioning like "some challenges have emerged around the proposed tier" is not kindness, it is fog, and the client pays you specifically to see through fog. "The $15 tier loses money at current churn" is more respectful, because it treats the reader as someone who can hear it.

AI's proper role here is rehearsal partner, never sender. Ask for three versions - too soft, too blunt, and right - and read all three: the spread teaches you where the line sits. Then role-play the reply: "respond as a CEO who has championed this tier for a year." You will meet Thursday's objections on Tuesday. The final draft is yours, sent under your name, ideally trailed by a phone call. One confidentiality note: the rehearsal prompt uses anonymized numbers and no client names - session b8 makes that reflex non-negotiable.

Real worldRecall the Deloitte Australia rail from b1: the firm refunded part of a A$439,000 contract not because its strategy was wrong but because unverified production details - fabricated references - reached the client. Difficult news is the highest-stakes text you will send all engagement, and it lives or dies on the evidence being checkable. Every number in the $15-tier email has a human owner: you. If the retention math is wrong, no amount of tact survives it.
Everyone drafts

Draft the three ✍ ★ 20 min · everyone works

No lab widget today - your keyboard is the lab. Three real drafts by end of session, each checked against the b5 measures by the scorer you carry in your head now.

Write the week-5 Northbridge status note. Four lines - done, next, risks, asks - with real case facts in them. "Done: pricing analysis complete, $30 recommendation locked" beats "made good progress on pricing."

Write the steering pre-read opening. Answer first: the $30 recommendation in sentence one, carried by its three numbers - 210 accounts already in procurement, 62% of trial teams hitting the seat cap, 94% vs 71% retention. The ask for Thursday's committee stated plainly.

Draft the difficult-news email about the $15 tier. Evidence (the retention math), alternative (the $30 tier against rivals at $38-45), directness (say "loses money at current churn" - no cushioning).

Run each draft through the b5 measures mentally. Answer in line one? Every claim quantified? Weasels gone? Cadence and its numbers actually named? The scorer is not here; its habits should be.

Read the difficult one aloud. Every sentence you would not say to the CEO's face gets deleted or rewritten. Expect to lose the first paragraph - almost everyone does.

Swap with a neighbor. Mark every sentence in their drafts that could have come from any consultant to any client. Whatever survives unmarked is voice; the rest is template. Compare counts.

✦ If you finish early Draft the same status note twice: once from your bullets, once by asking AI to write it with no input beyond "write a consulting status update." Put them side by side. The second one is what your client receives from firms that skipped this session.
Before next session

Homework 📝 ◐ 20 minutes

  • Rewrite your last real status email into the four-line shape - done, next, risks, asks - with a number in at least two lines. Compare the length: the four-line version is shorter and says more.
  • Find one "I hope this email finds you well" in your own sent folder (or its cousin), and rewrite the whole email in your speaking register using the read-aloud test.
  • Draft - do not send - a difficult-news email you actually owe someone, using evidence + alternative + directness. Notice which of the three you instinctively skip; that is your growth edge.
Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The steering pre-read exists so that...

A pre-read is the b5 pyramid in miniature: answer in sentence one, sent two days ahead. It converts meeting time from information transfer to decision making. A pre-read that buries the ask wastes the committee.

2 · AI's default email voice fails with clients because...

"I hope this email finds you well" carries zero client anchors, and the recipient's own AI probably wrote half their inbox in the same register. Voice = your firm's register + the client's vocabulary - both things you must feed in.

3 · Difficult news travels with...

Evidence the reader can check, an alternative so a door opens as one closes, and plain words - "some challenges have emerged" is worse than "the $15 tier loses money at current churn." AI rehearses the delivery; a human sends it.
