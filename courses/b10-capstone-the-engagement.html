Part 1 · covers the recap

The arc, one breath 6 min live

Nine stations, one engagement, each with a single takeaway worth keeping. Where a station has a signature number, it is on the map - 19 to 100 on the ladder, 8 in the slop trap, 15 at the gate. If any station feels foggy, its session is one click away; the capstone assumes them all.

LiveStation by station, one takeaway each4 min▶

Say these out loud with the map up - the recall is the exercise. b1: the frontier is jagged and invisible; map it, and obey the two rails that outrank every gain. b2: the hypothesis tree with explicit exclusions - what you will NOT investigate is half the scope. b3: an AI-supplied fact is a lead, not a fact, until a human sees its source; the log starts day one. b4: findings become insights without flattening - keep the outlier interviews the summary wants to average away. b5: the pyramid, and the ladder from 19 to 100 - answer first, quantify, so-what, anchor.

b6: the slop trap - the all-AI deck scored 8, the consultant-owned one 100, same tool, different division of labor. b7: the voice is yours, especially for difficult news. b8: the gate caps everything at 15 the moment client-identifying material appears, regardless of craft - an 87 on prose is still a 15 with a leak. b9: the citation audit (VERIFIED, VENDOR-CLAIMED, CUT) and the hostile-client rehearsal, then the readout: answer in two minutes, close with the ask.

Real worldThis compression exercise mirrors how firms actually onboard the tools. McKinsey's Lilli is used by roughly three quarters of its ~43,000 people at half a million prompts a month; BCG reports ~90% employee AI usage. At that saturation, using AI is not a differentiator - the sequence you just recited is. The consultants who compound are the ones for whom the rails, the ladder, and the gate run as one routine, not nine separate lessons.

LiveThe three habits that survive real deadlines3 min▶

Under deadline, checklists shrink to habits. If you keep only three from this whole course, keep these. Answer-first drafting: the recommendation in sentence one, always - it forces every other sentence to earn its place, and it is the single biggest lever the ladder ever showed you. Lead, not fact: every AI-supplied number is a lead until you have seen its source; the habit takes five seconds per fact and would have saved Deloitte Australia a headline. Anonymize, then prompt: strip client-identifying material before it enters any tool, even the approved one - the gate taught you that craft cannot buy back a leak.

Real worldThe jagged-frontier study is why these three, specifically. Bottom-half performers gained +43% with AI, and output homogenized - so fluent production is now table stakes that differentiates nobody. What the study could not automate is what the three habits protect: the judgment to commit to an answer, the accountability for every fact, and the client trust that confidentiality keeps. Those are the parts of the job that still compound.
Part 3 · covers what comes next

The jagged frontier, revisited 4 min live

In session 1 you drew a frontier map of your real task list: INSIDE, EDGE, OUTSIDE. Reread it now with nine sessions of hindsight - and notice what moved.

LiveReread your b1 map, then keep re-sounding it3 min▶

For most people, something migrated from EDGE to INSIDE - interview synthesis once you learned to keep the outliers, hostile-question generation once you learned the answer-with-source-row discipline, first-draft summaries once the six measures became your edit pass. The frontier did not move; your knowledge of where it runs did. That was the point: the study's real lesson was never "AI is good" or "AI is bad" but that knowing where the edge runs IS the consulting skill.

So the standing habit: re-sound the frontier quarterly. It moves - models improve, your tasks change, your firm's approved tools change. One hour, your current task list, three columns, honest EDGE entries. And when the day comes that you run the practice instead of the workstream, the leader track is waiting: a1 for the evidence, a3 for the policy anatomy behind the rails, a5 for the firm's AI operating model.

The ladder + your draft ▶ ★ 25 min · everyone scores their own

Three presses replay the ladder's key rungs one last time - then the box opens for YOUR text. The drafts are scripted rewrites, the measures are real pattern checks, and nothing pasted leaves the browser. That includes your real summary.

1 · Press "1 · Intern draft" - it scores 19. Before you look at the table, name the three missing measures from memory. Then check. If you named answer-first, quantified support, and client anchors without hesitating, the course did its job.

2 · Press "2 · Answer + numbers" - it scores 78. Say out loud which two moves bought those 59 points: the answer moved to sentence one, and every claim took its number. Two moves, 59 points - the ladder's whole economics in one press.

3 · Press "3 · So-what + anchors" - it scores 100. The ceiling. Notice why the anchors matter: so-whats a model can attempt, but the client anchors - Cadence named, the 210-account mechanics, the bottleneck called - are the points AI cannot supply, because your client's specifics live in your engagement, not its training data.

4 · Now the box. Paste YOUR OWN executive summary - the rewrite from b5's homework, or write one right now for a real engagement you are on. Press "📏 Score my draft".

5 · Read the coaching line honestly. Under 50 is intern territory, 50-79 is a solid skeleton, 80+ is readout-grade. Then read the table under the number - the missing points are usually so-whats and anchors, the two measures that demand you draw consequences and name specifics.

6 · Fix ONE measure and re-score. Add the missing so-whats, or the client anchors, or move the answer to sentence one - just one move. Watch the number respond. That is the ladder working on YOUR text, and it is the edit loop you will run for the rest of your career.

7 · Note the gate still runs on pasted text. If the leak scan fires on your real summary, do not be embarrassed - you just learned something important for free, in a browser, instead of in a client meeting. Anonymize and re-score.

8 · The graduation sentence. The scorer stays here, free, forever. Come back before your next readout - paste, score, fix one measure, ship.

✦ If you finish early Score a colleague's summary (with their blessing) and practice delivering the coaching read out loud - band first, then the one measure to fix. Teaching the ladder is the retention trick, and it is also tonight's homework.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The nine stations of the engagement, in order, are...

The arc follows the six weeks: meet the engagement, scope it, research it, analyze it, structure the story, build the deck, manage the client, protect the data, then audit and present. The deck never precedes the storyline, and the audit always precedes the room.

2 · The three keep-forever habits are...

The answer in sentence one forces every other sentence to earn its place; every AI fact stays a lead until a human sees its source; client-identifying material gets stripped before any prompt. Three habits, and every headline incident this course cited traces to skipping one of them.

3 · The paste box's coaching bands say...

The bands grade craft signals, not truth - the citation audit guards the facts. And the pattern holds on real text: drafts most often bleed points on consequences never drawn and clients never named, the two measures AI cannot supply for you.
