Part 1 · covers the evidence base

The jagged frontier: what the data actually says 8 min live

In 2023, Harvard researchers ran 758 BCG consultants - about 7% of the firm's individual contributors - through realistic consulting tasks, with and without GPT-4. It remains the largest field experiment on AI-assisted consulting work, and its two headline findings point in opposite directions. Both belong in your head permanently.

LiveCentaurs, cyborgs, and where the skill moved4 min▶

The study's authors describe two patterns among consultants who navigated the frontier well. Centaurs divide the work: discrete subtasks go to the AI (draft this, summarize that), and the human keeps every decision point. Cyborgs weave AI through the whole workflow continuously. Both work; what fails is the third, unnamed pattern - pasting the task in and shipping whatever comes back.

Two second-order findings matter as much as the headline. First, the bottom half gained most (+43% vs +17%): AI is a floor-raiser, which means baseline production stops differentiating anyone. Second, output homogenized - AI-assisted answers converged on similar ideas. Put those together and the conclusion writes itself: the parts of consulting AI does well are becoming table stakes, and the parts it cannot do - judgment, framing, client specificity, accountability - are where careers now compound. The scorer you meet in session 5 measures exactly those.

Real worldThis is not theoretical adoption. McKinsey's internal genAI platform Lilli (launched 2023, named for Lillian Dombrowski, the firm's first professional woman hire) is used by roughly three quarters of its ~43,000 people, at half a million prompts a month, with the firm claiming about 30% time saved on information gathering. BCG reports ~90% employee AI usage, with internal tools for slide drafting. PwC bought ChatGPT Enterprise for over 100,000 employees and became OpenAI's first reseller. The frontier question is not whether your firm uses AI - it is whether you know where its edge runs.
Self-studyHow to read "-19 percentage points" honestly2 min▶

The outside-frontier task was chosen precisely because AI would get it subtly wrong - the researchers built a trap on purpose, the way session 5's scorer builds one. The honest reading is not "AI fails 19% of the time"; it is "on tasks beyond its capability, AI-assisted professionals did WORSE than unassisted ones, because fluent wrong answers are more persuasive than no answer." Every task you delegate this course, you will first ask: is this inside the frontier, and how would I know if it were not? That question, asked habitually, is worth more than any prompt technique.

Part 2 · covers the non-negotiables

The two rails, previewed 6 min live

Two rules outrank every productivity gain in this course, each anchored to a real incident that cost a real firm money and reputation. Today you learn them as headlines; session a3 gives leaders the policy anatomy, and sessions b3 and b8 drill them into your hands.

LiveRail 1: client material never enters an untrusted tool3 min▶

In spring 2023, Samsung permitted ChatGPT internally; within about twenty days, employees had pasted confidential source code and an internal meeting transcript into it, and by May the company had banned generative AI on work devices. The consulting version of that incident is worse - the leaked material belongs to your CLIENT, and the trust you burn is the product you sell.

The working rule has an architectural half and a behavioral half. Architectural: consumer AI tiers may train on your conversations unless you opt out - the vendors say so themselves - while enterprise and API tiers of the major vendors do not train on business data by default, contractually. Firms buy enterprise tools precisely to make the guarantee contractual instead of behavioral. Behavioral: even inside approved tools, client-identifying material gets anonymized before it enters a prompt. Session b8 makes you practice both until they are reflexes.

LiveRail 2: every fact in a deliverable has a human owner3 min▶

In October 2025, Deloitte Australia partially refunded the Australian government for a A$439,000 report after an academic found fabricated references and a misattributed court quote - errors Deloitte attributed to generative AI use it later disclosed. The refund was modest; the headline was not. Every consulting firm on earth now has a version of the same clause: a human is accountable for every citation, every quote, every number that reaches a client.

The consulting cousin of the fake legal case (which learn-ai-law covers in depth) is the invented statistic - plausible, well-formatted, and nonexistent. Session b3 teaches the source-log discipline that catches it; session b9 makes the citation audit a standing QA gate. For now, one habit: an AI-supplied fact is a lead, not a fact, until you have seen its source with your own eyes.

Real worldNote what the Deloitte incident did NOT involve: bad strategy, wrong conclusions, or a hostile client. The analysis may well have been fine. The firm refunded and made the news because of un-verified production details - exactly the work most consultants now delegate most casually. The rails exist because the cheap-looking work carries the expensive risk.

Everyone sets up

Your governed setup + frontier map 🗺 ★ 20 min · everyone works

No lab widget today - the setup IS the exercise. Two artifacts by end of session: a toolkit you could defend to your firm's risk team, and a frontier map of your real workload.

Inventory your tools. List every AI tool you have touched for work in the last month. Mark each: consumer tier or enterprise/API tier? If you do not know, that IS the answer - find out this week.

Write your two-line data rule. Line one: which tool tier client material may enter (enterprise/approved only). Line two: what gets anonymized even there (names, figures, anything identifying). Steal session b8's version later; write your own first.

List your last ten work tasks. Real ones - the proposal section, the interview summary, the competitor scan, the awkward email.

Frontier-map them. Three columns: INSIDE (AI drafts well, you review - summaries, restructures, first passes), EDGE (AI helps but has failed you here before - anything requiring facts it must fetch), OUTSIDE (judgment, politics, commitments). Be honest about the EDGE column; that is where the -19 points live.

Pick your delegation for this course. One INSIDE task you will consistently hand to AI from now on, and one OUTSIDE task you commit never to delegate. Write both down - the capstone asks whether you kept the contract.

Read the Cadence facts once more. By session 5 the scorer will expect them in your writing from memory - 62%, 210, 94 vs 71, $38-45, $30, 2.1x.

✦ If you finish early Ask your favorite AI tool a question about YOUR industry whose answer you already know cold. Grade it. You just took one sounding of where the frontier runs in your domain - the habit, repeated, is the skill.

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · The BCG field study found consultants using GPT-4 were faster and better inside the frontier - and on the task outside it...

The dangerous part is that the wrong answers read as confident and polished as the right ones. Fluent wrong beats absent wrong at persuading people - which is why frontier-mapping is the skill.

2 · The clean version of the confidentiality rail is...

Architectural half: enterprise/API tiers do not train on business data by default - a contractual guarantee. Behavioral half: anonymize anyway. Deleting a consumer chat does not undo what the terms permitted at submission.

3 · Deloitte Australia's 2025 partial refund happened because...

The analysis was not the problem - unverified production details were. Every fact reaching a client needs a human owner who has seen its source; that is Rail 2, and session b9 makes it a standing QA gate.
